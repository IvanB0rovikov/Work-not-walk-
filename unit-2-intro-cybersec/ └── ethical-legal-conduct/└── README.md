### Scenario 1: The colleague's password

**What is happening (in my own words):**
My colleague is asking me to log in to his account from my device and view the client's data.

**What's legally at stake:**
The legal risks stem from the fact that I would be using someone else's account—even with their permission—and accessing confidential client data.

**What's ethically at stake:**
The ethical dilemma is that a colleague is asking me for a favor, and refusing might imply that I don't want to help him—or, in other words, that I don't care about his problems. In reality, however, I simply don't want to put either of us in a compromising position.

**What I would do:**
That’s a tricky question because it really depends on the individual situation. On one hand, there’s a colleague I know and work with every day; failing to help him could potentially damage our relationship. On the other hand, there’s a client I’ve never met or spoken to—someone I’m essentially indifferent toward. Yet, I have to realize that accessing a client’s confidential information—when it wasn't part of my assigned duties—could lead to criminal liability. Frankly speaking, I wouldn't do it; I’m not inclined to get close to people at work or school—they are colleagues, not friends.

**What I would NOT do, and why:**
Naturally, I won’t do what I was asked to do, even if a colleague says that it’s only for one time. I also won’t report it to management—at least not the first time around—because we’re only human and anyone can make a mistake; I’ll simply warn the colleague about the risks and advise them against doing anything like that. Although maybe I still don't care what happens to him

**Who I would consult:**
I wouldn't consult other colleagues, as that could lead to a scandal or even the employee's dismissal; instead, I would consult my grandfather, because I value his opinion, and my uncle, because he has extensive experience working in an IT company.
